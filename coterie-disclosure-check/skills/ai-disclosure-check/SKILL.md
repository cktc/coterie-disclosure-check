# AI disclosure check (APP 1.7 to 1.9)

This skill helps an Australian business with one obligation: from 10 December 2026, an
organisation covered by the Privacy Act must say in its privacy policy when software makes,
or substantially and directly shapes, decisions about people that could significantly affect
them. It does three jobs, in this order:

1. **Coverage check.** Does the duty likely apply? Six short questions.
2. **Decision list.** Which programs and tools make or shape decisions about people?
3. **Policy review.** Does the privacy policy cover what APP 1.8 requires, and what wording
   would fill the gaps?

Start where the person is. If they paste or attach a privacy policy, offer the review, but run
the coverage check first if they haven't done it: the review depends on its outcome.

## Before anything else

Read `references/rules.md`. It holds the checked questions, the outcome rules, what the
policy must cover and the sources. **Every legal statement you make must come from that file.**
If the person asks something it doesn't answer, say the check doesn't cover it and that it
is a question for their adviser.

Never state penalty amounts, enforcement priorities, or any date for removing the
small-business exemption. None of these could be confirmed at a primary source.

## 1. Coverage check

- Ask the questions in `references/rules.md` one at a time, in order, with the options as
  written. Show the help text where the question has it.
- Follow the skip rules in "which questions to ask". Stop early where they say to.
- If an answer doesn't fit an option, ask a short follow-up to place it. If it still doesn't
  fit, record `unsure`.
- Work out the outcome with the rules in "the outcome". Never reach a different outcome
  from your own reading of the law, even if you think it is better.
- Report: the outcome label and headline, then one line per answer saying what it means,
  then the sources cited for those answers.

If the outcome is `likely-not-yet`, say the decision list is still worth keeping and stop
unless the person wants to go on.

## 2. Decision list

Follow `references/decision-list.md`. Build the list with the person, one tool at a time,
in the table format from `references/rules.md`. Don't decide for them whether a tool is in
scope: mark what the answers suggest and flag the judgement calls.

## 3. Policy review

Follow `references/policy-review.md`. The review checks the policy against APP 1.8's three
items and the OAIC's drafting points, then drafts wording for the gaps from the template in
`references/rules.md`.

## How to write

- Plain Australian English, short sentences. Write for a practice owner, not a lawyer.
- Say "likely", never "you must" or "you comply". The result is a reading of their answers,
  not a ruling on their business.
- Cite the source in brackets after each legal point, for example "[OAIC fact sheet]".
- Keep everything in this conversation. This skill sends nothing anywhere and needs no tools.
