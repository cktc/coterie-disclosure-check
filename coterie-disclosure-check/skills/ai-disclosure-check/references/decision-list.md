# Building the decision list

The decision list is the evidence behind the privacy policy. Each row is one program or tool
that makes, recommends, scores or ranks something about a person, using information about
that person. Use the columns in `rules.md`.

## How to run it

1. Ask what software the business uses with clients, prospects or applicants. Prompt with
   the "where to look" list in `rules.md`, then with the examples below for their kind of firm.
2. For each tool, ask the column questions in order: what decision it makes or informs, what
   personal information it uses, whether it decides alone or a person decides, and whether
   the decision could significantly affect the person.
3. Fill one row per tool. Where an answer is unknown, write "ask the vendor" or "check".
4. After the table, list the rows that turn on a judgement call, with the reason from
   `rules.md`. Typical calls: how much weight staff give a score, whether an effect is more
   than trivial, and decisions about the firm's own staff.

A tool belongs on the list only if it does something about a person. Software that only
drafts text, transcribes or stores files does not decide anything by itself. Ask what
happens to its output before leaving it off.

## Places to look, by kind of firm

These are prompts for the conversation, not findings. Whether a tool is in scope depends on
the answers to the column questions.

**Financial advice**

- Risk-profiling questionnaires that score a client's risk tolerance.
- Tools that screen or rank products or portfolios for a client.
- Client tiering or segmentation that sets service levels or fees.
- Identity and anti-money-laundering screening during onboarding.
- Insurance needs calculators and underwriting pre-assessment tools.
- CRM lead scoring or next-best-action prompts.

**Accounting and bookkeeping**

- Client acceptance or risk screening at onboarding.
- Credit terms, payment plans or debt-collection order set by software.
- Fee or pricing calculators applied to clients.
- Identity and anti-money-laundering screening.

**Legal**

- Intake or conflict screening that accepts or declines matters.
- Triage that sets priority or routes matters to a level of service.
- Fee estimates generated from client details.

**Any business**

- Phone agents and chatbots that sort, route or decline people.
- Booking tools that set who gets which slot.
- Spreadsheets that rank or score people. A formula counts as a computer program.

## Output

Give the table, then the judgement calls, then one line on what the list means for the
policy: decisions in the "decides alone" column go in the first list of the draft wording,
and decisions where a person decides with the tool's help go in the second.
