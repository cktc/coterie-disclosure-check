# Reviewing a privacy policy

The review tells the person whether their privacy policy covers the three items APP 1.8
requires, where it falls short, and what wording would fill the gaps. It reads only what the
person gives you.

## Before you start

- Get the coverage-check outcome. If they haven't run it, run it first.
- If the outcome is `likely-not-yet`, say the review is optional and ask whether they want it.
- Ask for the decision list if they have one. Without it, ask which tools make or shape
  decisions about people, briefly, before reviewing. The review is only as good as this list.

## The review

1. **Find the relevant text.** Look for any section about automated decisions, AI, computer
   programs, algorithms, profiling, scoring or software that makes decisions. Quote it. If
   there is none, say so.
2. **Check each APP 1.8 item** from `rules.md`. For each one give a status:
   - **Covered**: the policy states it in terms meaningful to a reasonable person.
   - **Partly covered**: it is mentioned, but vaguely, or only for some of the tools on the list.
   - **Missing**: the policy doesn't state it.

   Quote the words you relied on. Explain any gap in one sentence.
3. **Check against the decision list.** Every kind of decision on the list should appear
   under the right heading: decisions made solely by a program in one place, decisions a
   program substantially and directly shapes in the other. Name each tool whose decision
   isn't covered.
4. **Check the OAIC's drafting points** from `rules.md`:
   - Is the detail meaningful to a reasonable person, not just "we may use technology"?
   - Is sensitive information, such as health details, called out where it is used?
   - Is it free of technical detail that doesn't help the reader?
   - Are the two kinds of decision kept apart?
5. **Draft wording for the gaps.** Fill the template in `rules.md` from the decision list:
   the kinds of personal information, the decisions made solely by programs, and the
   decisions programs help a person make. Keep a bracket wherever a fact is unknown. Never
   invent a tool, a decision or a kind of information the person didn't give you.

## Output

Use this order:

1. One line: how many of the three items the policy covers, partly covers or misses.
2. A table with one row per APP 1.8 item: item, status, the policy's words, the gap.
3. Tools on the decision list that the policy doesn't cover.
4. Drafting points that need work, one line each.
5. The draft wording, in a block the person can copy, labelled as a draft for their adviser.
6. The wording notes from `rules.md`.
7. The footer from the skill.

## Limits to state when they apply

- The review covers APP 1.7 to 1.9 only, not the rest of the privacy policy or the Privacy Act.
- It can't see how the software is actually used. A policy that reads well can still be wrong
  about the business.
- Never say the policy "complies". Say it "covers the three items as far as this review can tell".
